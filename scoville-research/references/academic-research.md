# Academic research

Use this route when the answer depends on papers, publication status, methods, experiments, scholarly disagreement, or a literature landscape.

## Define the scholarly question

Turn the request into one research question and a small set of subquestions. Record the time window, fields and neighboring terminology, publication types, languages, and whether the user needs discovery, a narrative review, a reproducibility assessment, or an implementation bridge.

Do not call a result systematic, comprehensive, or meta-analytic unless the search and screening protocol actually meets that standard.

## Discover across indexes

Use several query variants and, where available, structured sources such as arXiv, Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, the relevant venue or university repository, and discipline-specific indexes. Deduplicate by DOI, arXiv identifier, title, and version relationship.

For central papers, follow both references and citing work. Related-work sections and review papers are discovery maps, not substitutes for the primary studies behind a claim.

## Inspect the evidence at its real depth

Record publication state precisely:

- peer-reviewed venue publication;
- accepted manuscript or author version;
- preprint;
- workshop, poster, thesis, technical report, or dataset paper;
- retracted, corrected, superseded, or unclear.

An abstract supports only abstract-level reporting. Before relying on methods, effect sizes, tables, figures, limitations, or ablations, inspect the relevant full-text section. Keep paper, code repository, dataset, model artifact, and project page as linked but separate sources.

## Compare studies without flattening them

Check population or dataset, task definition, baseline, metric, sample size, experimental setup, uncertainty, ablation, and stated limitations before combining results. A citation count is neither truth nor methodological quality. A later paper is not automatically a better paper.

When sources disagree, first check whether they studied the same construct under comparable conditions. If not, report the boundary. If they are comparable, preserve the disagreement and state which evidence is stronger and why, or leave it unresolved.

## Bridge research to implementation

When the user needs implementation options, connect each paper to available code, data, license, reproducibility evidence, hardware or data requirements, and the gap between the reported experiment and the user's environment. Do not present a paper's existence as proof that its method is production-ready.

## Report

State the answer, publication-status boundary, strongest supporting and contradicting evidence, methodological limitations, open questions, and implementation relevance. Cite the exact paper version inspected and identify abstract-only coverage where full text was not examined.
